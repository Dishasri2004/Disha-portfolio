WHY HUMAN-CENTERED DESIGN?

Start with people, not screens.

A design assumption is not a user insight. I want to understand context, make assumptions visible, and validate the parts that matter.

01

Understand context

Look beyond the happy path: devices, language, connectivity, stress, routines, and the support people rely on.

02

Reduce cognitive load

Use plain language, clear hierarchy, one primary next step, and feedback that helps people recover from errors.

03

Design for difference

Consider digital confidence, literacy, age, ability, shared devices, and the realities of low-resource settings.

04

Validate assumptions

Use research and usability studies to learn what is true before treating a polished interface as a solution.

Design hypothesis

Proposed research

Test this hypothesis through semi-structured interviews, a short survey, contextual inquiry, and moderated usability sessions.

MY DESIGN APPROACH

From understanding the problem to building the experience.

01

Understand

Explore needs, context, constraints, and existing behavior.

02

Frame

Turn evidence and assumptions into a focused problem definition.

03

Structure

Organize information and workflows around the user’s next decision.

04

Prototype

Make the experience tangible in Figma and testable before build.

05

Build together

Discuss feasibility with engineers and preserve the intent of the design.

06

Reflect

Document what changed, what remains uncertain, and what to learn next.

ACCESSIBILITY BY DEFAULT

Small interface choices can remove large barriers.

A practical checklist I bring into structure, visual design, and implementation.

PerceivableReadable type, sufficient contrast, text alternatives, and information that does not rely on color alone.
OperableKeyboard access, visible focus, large touch targets, predictable navigation, and reduced motion.
UnderstandablePlain language, meaningful labels, prevention before errors, and actionable validation messages.
RobustSemantic HTML, heading structure, screen-reader-friendly controls, and progressive enhancement.
Needs improvement
